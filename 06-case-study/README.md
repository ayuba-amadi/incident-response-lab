# Task 6 — Healthcare Ransomware Case Study and Critical Reflection

## 1. Overview

This directory contains the healthcare ransomware case-study
analysis prepared for **Task 6 of the ICDFA GRC105 Incident
Response Planning and Ransomware Response Simulation assignment**.

The case is drawn from the healthcare example presented in
the ICDFA Week 20 Incident Response Planning teaching material,
specifically slide 16.

The objective is to evaluate the described incident response,
identify strengths and limitations, and apply relevant lessons
to the fictional Northbridge Skills Institute ransomware scenario.

This directory contains academic analysis rather than
documentation of an independently investigated hospital incident.

## 4. Source and analytical boundaries

The primary instructional basis is the healthcare
ransomware case included in the Week 20 teaching material.

This repository distinguishes three categories:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Case information | Details described in the original teaching case |
| Analytical interpretation | Conclusions reasoned from the case information |
| Northbridge application | Improvements proposed for the fictional educational institute |

These categories should not be conflated.

The analysis does not independently establish the identity
of an affected healthcare organisation, the precise forensic
cause of an attack, or any finding absent from the teaching case.

## 5. Required assessment components

The completed case-study analysis addresses the following
assessment areas.

### 5.1 Three incident response strengths

The analysis evaluates three favourable aspects of the
healthcare response described in the teaching example.

The precise strengths and supporting reasoning are presented
in the [case-study analysis](TASK-6-CASE-STUDY-ANALYSIS.md).

### 5.2 Two limitations or unresolved issues

The assessment considers two areas in which the response
may have been constrained or where the available case
information does not establish a complete outcome.

A limitation identified for discussion must not automatically
be treated as proof of negligence or procedural failure.

### 5.3 Two improvements transferable to Northbridge

The case is used to inform two relevant improvements for
the Northbridge ransomware response framework.

Transferability is considered in the context of Northbridge's
limited IT staffing, affected learning services, backup
uncertainty and examination-continuity needs.

### 5.4 Critical restoration versus full recovery

The case-study task distinguishes restoration of critical
services within **72 hours** from wider recovery completed
over **seven days**.

These milestones represent different recovery scopes.

Restoring priority services does not necessarily establish
that all systems, records, dependencies, security checks
and business processes have returned to normal operation.

### 5.5 Evidence needed before excluding data theft

The analysis considers what evidence would be necessary
before making a defensible statement that patient data
was not stolen.

An absence of confirmed theft is not equivalent to
forensic proof that theft did not occur.

Relevant conclusions must remain proportionate to the
available investigation results and evidence quality.

### 5.6 Reconsidering a Northbridge decision

The task connects the case-study lessons to one Northbridge
decision reconsidered after the fictional tabletop exercise.

The revised decision is explained in the authoritative
case-study narrative rather than recreated in this README.

## 7. Critical analytical principles

The case-study assessment follows several safeguards:

1. Preserve the original case's factual limits.
2. Separate response achievements from unresolved findings.
3. Distinguish critical-service restoration from full recovery.
4. Require appropriate evidence for claims concerning data theft.
5. Avoid introducing unsupported healthcare incident details.
6. Adapt lessons to Northbridge's operational circumstances.
7. Preserve the distinction between real case discussion and
   fictional Northbridge exercise decisions.
